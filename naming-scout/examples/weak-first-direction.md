# Example: the first direction is wrong

The first territory had a *better* availability rate than the one that produced the shortlist.
Following the rate would have produced free domains and bad names.

*Checks run 2026-09-08. Re-run before deciding.*

---

**User:** A paid community for lawyers leaving corporate law. Peer support, job leads, people
who've already done it. Should feel like it's for grown-ups. These are people earning $300k
who are scared.

**Scout:** Checks: domain, social handles, search, trademark because it is paid. No technical
registries.

One question. Should the name say "law", or should it be recognisable only to the people it is
for?

**User:** Definitely not "law". That's the point, they're leaving.

## Round 1: exit, escape, leaving

I generated here first because it is where the brief points. That was a mistake.

**3 of 12 free.**

```
formercounsel.com    free       lastbillable.com   free
offrampcounsel.com   free
theofframp.com       for_sale   2011, Atom
escapelaw.com        parked     2016
exitcounsel.com      registered 2020    lawexit.com    registered 2017
```

25% is a workable rate. **The territory still failed, and availability had nothing to do with
it.**

Every name defines the audience by what they are leaving. ExLawyer. PostLaw. LawExit.
FormerCounsel. A community named after the thing its members are running from makes leaving
the permanent identity. For people described as scared and proud, that is precisely wrong.
Nobody wants to join Former Counsel. They want to join something that looks like where they
are going.

The user had also ruled out "law" in the name. Half of round 1 contains "law" or "counsel". I
generated from the brief's vocabulary instead of its meaning.

The brand filter caught this, not the domain check. No candidate could pass the one-sentence
test without sounding like a redundancy notice.

## Round 2: courtroom words used sideways

New premise. Use the audience's professional vocabulary for something other than its literal
meaning. It signals membership without saying "law", and the name describes a room rather than
a departure.

**2 of 14 free.**

```
thelongrecess.com   free            strikethejury.com   free
thesidebar.com      for_sale  2012, Afternic
theretainer.com     for_sale  2019, Afternic
secondchair.com     registered 1999    offtherecord.com  registered 1997
voirdire.com        registered 1996    sidebarclub.com   registered 2024
```

Worse availability, much better names. The two facts are unrelated.

- **Sidebar.** In court, a sidebar is when counsel approaches the bench to speak privately,
  off the record, away from the jury. That is what a peer community is.
- **Second chair.** The junior attorney at trial. Carries a supporting role and a second act
  at once.
- **The long recess.** A recess is a pause, not an ending. For someone terrified that leaving
  is permanent failure, an intermission is a different proposition entirely.

## Round 3: stem variants

Also tested "hang a shingle", the idiom for starting your own practice, which points at the
destination rather than the departure.

**2 of 12 free.** `ownshingle.com` and `theshinglelist.com`. `thesecondchair.com` is on
NameBright. Nothing beat round 2, and the compounds are all worse than the stems. Stopping
here.

## Shortlist

**The Long Recess** (`.com` free)
A recess is a pause the court calls. Everyone in this audience knows the word in that exact
sense. It reframes leaving as an interval rather than a defeat, which is the most useful thing
a name can do for someone in this position. Never says "law" and is unmistakably legal.
*Weakness:* "recess" is also school playtime, and for a US audience that association is strong
enough to undercut the grown-up tone. "The long" also implies a duration some members will
read as discouraging.

**Sidebar** (`thesidebar.com` for sale, held since 2012)
The best concept by some distance. Private counsel, off the record, out of earshot. It
describes what the product does rather than what it is about, so it survives if the community
broadens.
*Weakness:* web design and publishing have colonised the word, so it is crowded outside law
and you will fight for search. `sidebar.com` is long gone. Someone registered `sidebarclub.com`
in 2024, so the idea is occurring to other people.

**Second Chair** (`.com` registered 1999)
The supporting role at trial, and the second act of a career. Dignified and specific. It
flatters the member instead of labelling them.
*Weakness:* "second" implies a ranking. Second-best, second choice. That works against an
audience already afraid they are failing.

**Strike the Jury** (`.com` free)
Included to show what the territory produces at its edges. Memorable and unmistakably legal.
*Weakness:* it means removing a juror for bias, which has nothing to do with the community's
purpose. "Strike" is aggressive where the product is supportive. Good sound, wrong meaning.
Not recommended.

## Defensive sweep

Run on The Long Recess, the only one with a free `.com`.

```
thelongrecess.com   free   ← register this
thelongreces.com    free   ← the likely misspelling, $12
```

The rest of the sweep is free too. Nobody types `thethelongrecess`.

Not run on Sidebar or Second Chair. Their `.com` domains are taken, so a sweep would measure a
perimeter around a building you do not own.

## Top 3

1. **Sidebar.** The best name. It costs an aftermarket purchase and a permanent search
   problem. Take it if the concept matters more than the ranking.
2. **The Long Recess.** The one I would launch. Free `.com`, clean defensive perimeter, and
   the reframe is worth more to this audience than Sidebar's sharper concept.
3. **Second Chair.** Take it if the audience skews senior and "second act" beats "second best".

**Boldest bet:** none recommended. Strike the Jury is the only unusual name and its meaning
points the wrong way.

**Safest bet:** The Long Recess. Free domain, full sweep available, no visible trademark
collision, no format lock if it becomes a podcast or a conference.

## The point of this run

Round 1 scored 25% availability. Round 2 scored 14%. The shortlist came from round 2.

If availability had driven the decision, this run would have ended with FormerCounsel and
LastBillable. Both free. Both wrong.

Availability tells you where there is room. It never tells you what is good.

## Check these yourself

- Trademark not searched. It is required here. This is a paid product, and "Sidebar" is used
  commercially in adjacent fields. Get an attorney search before you take payment.
- Social handles not checked. `@sidebar` is certainly gone everywhere. Plan the fallback.
- "Sidebar" and "second chair" both return heavy generic results. "The long recess" is
  comparatively clear, which helps a community that will be found by name.
