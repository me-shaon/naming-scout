# Example: the first direction is weak, and the run has to change territory

Shows: recognising a dead territory from the names rather than from the availability rate,
pivoting, and running a defensive sweep on a finalist.

*Checks run 2026-09-08. Re-run before deciding.*

---

**User:** A paid community for lawyers leaving corporate law. Peer support, job leads,
people who've already done it. Name should feel like it's for grown-ups — these are people
earning $300k who are scared.

**Scout:** Domain, social handles, search, and a trademark check because it is paid. No
technical registries. One question: should the name say "law", or should it be recognisable
only to the people it is for?

**User:** Definitely not "law" in the name. That's the point — they're leaving.

## Round 1 — the literal territory: exit, escape, leaving

I generated here first because it is where the brief points. That was a mistake, and it is
worth showing why.

```
3 / 12 available   (25%)
```

```
formercounsel.com    available
lastbillable.com     available
offrampcounsel.com   available
theofframp.com       for_sale   2011    Atom
quitlaw.com          for_sale   2005    Afternic
escapelaw.com        parked     2016    CashParking
exitcounsel.com      registered 2020    lawexit.com      registered 2017
leavebiglaw.com      registered 2026    postlaw.com      registered 2002
```

25% is a workable availability rate. **The territory still failed, and availability had
nothing to do with it.** This is the case the rate cannot tell you about.

The names are bad for a specific reason: every one of them defines the audience by what
they are leaving. `ExLawyer`, `PostLaw`, `LawExit`, `FormerCounsel`, `LastBillable` — a
community named after the thing its members are running from makes leaving the permanent
identity. For an audience the user described as scared and proud, that is precisely wrong.
People do not want to join Former Counsel. They want to join something that already looks
like where they are going.

The user also ruled out "law" in the name, and half of round 1 has "law" or "counsel" in it.
I generated inside the constraint I had just been given, which is what happens when you take
the brief's vocabulary instead of its meaning.

**Diagnosis: the territory is conceptually wrong, not exhausted.** Availability would not
have caught this. The brand filter did — no candidate could pass the one-sentence test
without the sentence sounding like a redundancy notice.

## Round 2 — pivot: courtroom vocabulary used sideways

Different premise. Instead of naming the exit, use the audience's own professional
vocabulary for something other than its literal meaning. It signals membership without
saying "law", and it lets the name be about a room rather than a departure.

```
2 / 14 available   (14%)
```

```
thelongrecess.com   available     strikethejury.com   available
thesidebar.com      for_sale   2012   Afternic
theretainer.com     for_sale   2019   Afternic
secondchair.com     registered 1999   offtherecord.com  registered 1997
voirdire.com        registered 1996   thedocket.com     registered 2002
ofcounsel.com       registered 2001   therecess.com     registered 2009
sidebarclub.com     registered 2024   chambersclub.com  registered 2019
```

14% — worse availability, much better names. The two facts are unrelated and the second one
is what matters.

The standouts:

- **Sidebar** — in a courtroom, a sidebar is when counsel approaches the bench to speak
  privately, off the record, away from the jury. That is exactly what a peer community is:
  the conversation you cannot have in public. Best concept in the run.
- **Second chair** — the junior attorney who assists at trial. Carries both "supporting
  role" and "second act". Two meanings, both on target.
- **The long recess** — a recess is a pause in proceedings, not an ending. For someone
  terrified that leaving is permanent failure, framing it as an intermission is a genuinely
  different emotional proposition from anything round 1 produced.

## Round 3 — stem variants and a third territory

Testing whether the strong stems survive as compounds, plus "hang a shingle" (the idiom for
starting your own practice), which points at the destination rather than the departure.

```
2 / 12 available   (17%)
```

```
ownshingle.com       available     theshinglelist.com   available
thesecondchair.com   for_sale   2017   NameBright
newbrief.com         for_sale   2013   NameBright
hangashingle.com     registered 2020   theshingle.com     registered 2008
secondchairs.com     registered 2018   afterthebar.com    registered 2013
shingleclub.com      registered 2025   thesmallshop.com   registered 1999
```

The shingle territory produced nothing better than round 2 and the compounds are all worse
than the stems. Stopping here: three rounds, one strong territory, and no reason to think a
fourth would beat it.

## Shortlist

**The Long Recess** — *Courtroom, sideways* — `.com` available
A recess is a pause the court calls, not an ending, and everyone in this audience knows the
word in that precise sense. It reframes leaving as an interval rather than a defeat, which
is the single most useful thing a name can do for someone in this position. Warm without
being soft, and it never says "law" while being unmistakably legal.
*Weakness:* "recess" is also school playtime, and for a US audience that association is
strong enough to undercut the grown-up tone the user asked for. Three words, and "the long"
implies a duration that some members will read as discouraging.

**Sidebar** — *Courtroom, sideways* — `thesidebar.com` for_sale (Afternic, since 2012)
The best concept here by some distance: private counsel, off the record, out of earshot.
It describes the product's actual function rather than its subject matter, which means it
survives if the community broadens.
*Weakness:* "sidebar" has been thoroughly colonised by web design and by publishing, so the
term is crowded outside law and you will fight for search. `sidebar.com` is long gone;
`thesidebar.com` is an aftermarket purchase, and `sidebarclub.com` was registered by
someone else in 2024, which suggests the idea is occurring to other people right now.

**Second Chair** — *Courtroom, sideways* — `.com` registered 1999, `thesecondchair.com` for_sale
Two meanings that both land: the supporting role at trial, and the second act of a career.
Dignified, specific, and it flatters the member rather than labelling them.
*Weakness:* "second" carries a ranking implication — second-best, second choice — that
works against an audience already worried they are failing. The `.com` has been held since
1999 and the aftermarket alternative is a `the`-prefixed compound.

**Strike the Jury** — *Courtroom, sideways* — `.com` available
Included because it is available and because it shows what the territory produces at its
edges. A striking phrase, memorable, unmistakably legal.
*Weakness:* it means removing a juror for bias, which has nothing to do with the community's
purpose, and "strike" is aggressive in a way that reads as combative rather than supportive.
Good sound, wrong meaning. Not recommended.

**Own Shingle** · **The Shingle List** — available, from round 3, both weaker. `Own Shingle`
points at solo practice specifically, which is only one of the destinations this community
serves.

## Defensive sweep — finalist only

Run on The Long Recess, since it is the one with a free `.com`:

```
thelongrecess.com      available   ← the one to register
thelongreces.com       available   ← common misspelling, worth $12
getthelongrecess.com   available   jointhelongrecess.com   available
trythelongrecess.com   available   thethelongrecess.com    available
```

The whole defensive surface is open, which is normal for a phrase name and is one of their
underrated advantages. Register `thelongrecess.com` and `thelongreces.com`. The rest are
not worth buying — nobody types `thethelongrecess`.

Not run on Sidebar or Second Chair: their `.com`s are not available, so a defensive sweep
would be measuring a perimeter around a building you do not own.

## Top 3

1. **Sidebar** — the best name, and it costs an aftermarket purchase plus a permanent
   search-visibility problem. Pick it if the concept matters more than the ranking.
2. **The Long Recess** — the one I would actually launch. Free `.com`, clean defensive
   perimeter, and the reframe it performs is worth more to this specific audience than
   Sidebar's sharper concept.
3. **Second Chair** — the most dignified and the most conventional. Pick it if the audience
   skews senior and the "second act" reading dominates the "second best" one.

**Best unconventional:** none recommended. Strike the Jury is the only genuinely unusual
name in the run and its meaning points the wrong way. Round 1 produced nothing worth
promoting into the slot either.

**Safest:** The Long Recess. Free domain, full defensive sweep available, no trademark
collision visible, no format lock if it becomes a podcast or a conference.

## What this run demonstrates

The first territory had a **better availability rate** than the one that produced the
shortlist — 25% against 14%. If availability had driven the decision, the run would have
ended with `FormerCounsel` and `LastBillable`, both free, both wrong.

Availability tells you where there is room. It never tells you what is good.

## Not verified

- Trademark not yet searched. Required here — this is a paid product and "Sidebar" in
  particular is used commercially in adjacent fields. Get an attorney search before taking
  payment under any of these names.
- Social handles not checked. `@sidebar` is certainly gone everywhere; plan the fallback.
- Search: "sidebar" and "second chair" both return heavy generic results. "The long recess"
  is comparatively clear, which is a real advantage for a community that will be found by
  name.
