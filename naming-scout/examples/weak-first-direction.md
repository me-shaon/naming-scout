# Example: the first direction is weak and the run has to change territory

Shows a dead territory spotted from the names rather than from the availability rate. Shows
the pivot. Shows a defensive sweep on a finalist.

*Checks run 2026-09-08. Re-run before deciding.*

---

**User:** A paid community for lawyers leaving corporate law. Peer support, job leads, people
who've already done it. Name should feel like it's for grown-ups. These are people earning
$300k who are scared.

**Scout:** Checks: domain, social handles, search, and trademark because it is paid. No
technical registries.

One question. Should the name say "law", or should it be recognisable only to the people it
is for?

**User:** Definitely not "law" in the name. That's the point, they're leaving.

## Round 1: the literal territory. Exit, escape, leaving

I generated here first because it is where the brief points. That was a mistake and it is
worth showing why.

```
3 / 12 free   (25%)
```

```
formercounsel.com    free
lastbillable.com     free
offrampcounsel.com   free
theofframp.com       for_sale   2011    Atom
quitlaw.com          for_sale   2005    Afternic
escapelaw.com        parked     2016    CashParking
exitcounsel.com      registered 2020    lawexit.com      registered 2017
leavebiglaw.com      registered 2026    postlaw.com      registered 2002
```

25% is a workable rate. **The territory still failed, and availability had nothing to do with
it.** This is the case the rate cannot tell you about.

The names are bad for one specific reason. Every one defines the audience by what they are
leaving. ExLawyer. PostLaw. LawExit. FormerCounsel. LastBillable. A community named after the
thing its members are running from makes leaving the permanent identity. The user described
this audience as scared and proud. That framing is exactly wrong for them. Nobody wants to
join Former Counsel. They want to join something that already looks like where they are
going.

The user had also ruled out "law" in the name. Half of round 1 contains "law" or "counsel". I
generated inside the brief's vocabulary instead of its meaning.

**Diagnosis: the territory is conceptually wrong, not exhausted.** Availability would never
have caught this. The brand filter did. No candidate could pass the one-sentence test without
the sentence sounding like a redundancy notice.

## Round 2: courtroom vocabulary used sideways

Different premise. Instead of naming the exit, use the audience's own professional vocabulary
for something other than its literal meaning. It signals membership without saying "law", and
it lets the name describe a room rather than a departure.

```
2 / 14 free   (14%)
```

```
thelongrecess.com   free          strikethejury.com   free
thesidebar.com      for_sale   2012   Afternic
theretainer.com     for_sale   2019   Afternic
secondchair.com     registered 1999   offtherecord.com  registered 1997
voirdire.com        registered 1996   thedocket.com     registered 2002
ofcounsel.com       registered 2001   therecess.com     registered 2009
sidebarclub.com     registered 2024   chambersclub.com  registered 2019
```

14%. Worse availability, much better names. Those two facts are unrelated and the second one
is what matters.

- **Sidebar.** In a courtroom a sidebar is when counsel approaches the bench to speak
  privately, off the record, away from the jury. That is what a peer community is. The
  conversation you cannot have in public. Best concept in the run.
- **Second chair.** The junior attorney who assists at trial. It carries a supporting role and
  a second act at once.
- **The long recess.** A recess is a pause in proceedings, not an ending. For someone
  terrified that leaving is permanent failure, an intermission is a completely different
  proposition from anything round 1 produced.

## Round 3: stem variants and a third territory

Testing whether the strong stems survive as compounds. Added "hang a shingle", the idiom for
starting your own practice, which points at the destination rather than the departure.

The shingle territory produced nothing better than round 2. The compounds are all worse than
the stems. Stopping here. Three rounds, one strong territory, and no reason to think a fourth
would beat it.

## Shortlist

**The Long Recess** (Courtroom, sideways. `.com` free)
A recess is a pause the court calls, not an ending. Everyone in this audience knows the word
in that exact sense. It reframes leaving as an interval rather than a defeat, which is the
most useful thing a name can do for someone in this position. Warm without being soft. It
never says "law" and is unmistakably legal.
*Weakness:* "recess" is also school playtime. For a US audience that association is strong
enough to undercut the grown-up tone. Three words, and "the long" implies a duration some
members will read as discouraging.

**Sidebar** (Courtroom, sideways. `thesidebar.com` for_sale on Afternic, held since 2012)
The best concept here by some distance. Private counsel, off the record, out of earshot. It
describes what the product does rather than what it is about, so it survives if the community
broadens.
*Weakness:* web design and publishing have thoroughly colonised the word, so it is crowded
outside law and you will fight for search. `sidebar.com` is long gone. `thesidebar.com` is an
aftermarket purchase. Somebody else registered `sidebarclub.com` in 2024, which suggests the
idea is occurring to other people right now.

**Second Chair** (Courtroom, sideways. `.com` registered 1999, `thesecondchair.com` for_sale)
Two meanings that both land. The supporting role at trial, and the second act of a career.
Dignified and specific. It flatters the member instead of labelling them.
*Weakness:* "second" implies a ranking. Second-best, second choice. That works against an
audience already afraid they are failing. The `.com` has been held since 1999 and the
aftermarket alternative is a "the"-prefixed compound.

**Strike the Jury** (Courtroom, sideways. `.com` free)
Included because it is free and because it shows what the territory produces at its edges. A
striking phrase, memorable, unmistakably legal.
*Weakness:* it means removing a juror for bias, which has nothing to do with the community's
purpose. "Strike" is aggressive where the product is supportive. Good sound, wrong meaning.
Not recommended.

**Own Shingle and The Shingle List.** Free, from round 3, both weaker. Own Shingle points at
solo practice specifically, which is only one of the destinations this community serves.

## Defensive sweep, finalist only

Run on The Long Recess, since it is the one with a free `.com`:

```
thelongrecess.com      free   ← register this
thelongreces.com       free   ← common misspelling, worth $12
getthelongrecess.com   free   jointhelongrecess.com   free
trythelongrecess.com   free   thethelongrecess.com    free
```

The whole defensive surface is open, which is normal for a phrase name and is one of their
underrated advantages. Register `thelongrecess.com` and `thelongreces.com`. Skip the rest.
Nobody types `thethelongrecess`.

Not run on Sidebar or Second Chair. Their `.com` domains are not free, so a defensive sweep
would measure a perimeter around a building you do not own.

## Top 3

1. **Sidebar.** The best name. It costs an aftermarket purchase plus a permanent
   search-visibility problem. Take it if the concept matters more than the ranking.
2. **The Long Recess.** The one I would actually launch. Free `.com`, clean defensive
   perimeter, and the reframe it performs is worth more to this audience than Sidebar's
   sharper concept.
3. **Second Chair.** The most dignified and the most conventional. Take it if the audience
   skews senior and the "second act" reading dominates the "second best" one.

**Boldest bet:** none recommended. Strike the Jury is the only genuinely unusual name in the
run and its meaning points the wrong way. Round 1 produced nothing worth promoting either.

**Safest bet:** The Long Recess. Free domain, full defensive sweep available, no visible
trademark collision, no format lock if it becomes a podcast or a conference.

## What this run demonstrates

The first territory had a **better availability rate** than the one that produced the
shortlist. 25% against 14%. If availability had driven the decision, the run would have ended
with FormerCounsel and LastBillable. Both free. Both wrong.

Availability tells you where there is room. It never tells you what is good.

## Check these yourself

- Trademark has not been searched. It is required here. This is a paid product and "Sidebar"
  in particular is used commercially in adjacent fields. Get an attorney search before you
  take payment under any of these names.
- Social handles were not checked. `@sidebar` is certainly gone everywhere. Plan the fallback.
- Search: "sidebar" and "second chair" both return heavy generic results. "The long recess" is
  comparatively clear, which is a real advantage for a community that will be found by name.
