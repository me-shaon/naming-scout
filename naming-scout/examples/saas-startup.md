# Example: naming a startup

A founder with a product and no name. No developer checks. Two names get cut for colliding
with existing brands, which a domain check would never catch.

*Checks run 2026-09-09. States change. Re-run before deciding.*

---

**User:** I'm building a budgeting app for freelancers. The problem is irregular income. You
make £9k one month and £900 the next, so normal budgeting apps are useless. We smooth it out
and tell you what you can safely pay yourself each month. Need a name.

**Scout:** Three questions:

1. Who says the name out loud? A freelancer telling another freelancer, or you pitching
   investors? It changes how clever the name can afford to be.
2. Does it have to be `.com`? Would you buy one that is already registered?
3. For a consumer app I would check the domain, trademark, both app stores, social handles and
   search. No GitHub or npm, since nobody installs this from a terminal. Sound right?

**User:** Freelancers telling freelancers. `.com` ideally, and I'd pay a few thousand for the
right one. Checks sound right.

## Territories

The product turns a jagged income line into a flat one. Two ways to name that.

- **Steadiness at sea.** Ballast, even keel, trim, slack water.
- **Storing the harvest.** Granary, winter store, lean months. Older and more human. It is
  what freelancers already do without a name for it.
- **The float.** Money kept aside to cover the gap. Freelancers already use this word.
- **Naming the months.** Fat months, thin months, the month ahead.

## Round 1: steadiness at sea

**0 of 12 free on `.com`.**

```
evenkeel.com    registered 1997     ballast.com     registered 1995
trimtab.com     registered 1996     levelset.com    registered 1998
steadyhand.com  registered 2003     slackwater.com  registered 2004
```

Every one is a real English compound and they all went between 1995 and 2004. "Even Keel" is
the best name here and it has been held for nearly thirty years.

A permutation tool would now offer `evenkeelapp`, `getevenkeel`, `evenkeelhq`. Wrong move. The
territory is mined out, so change territory.

## Round 2: storing the harvest

**9 of 18 free on `.com`.**

```
evenmonths.com     free      leanmonths.com     free
harvestmonth.com   free      buffermonth.com    free
thelayby.com       free      betweenchecks.com  free
granary.com        for_sale  1994, Efty listing
```

From 0% to 50% by changing the metaphor. Two reasons this territory is open. The phrases are
longer, and no registrar tool generates `keepsomeback`. Storing grain for winter is also not a
fintech metaphor, so fintech has not stripped it.

## Shortlist

**Even Months**
States the outcome in two words. Irregular income made even. A freelancer gets it before you
finish the sentence. It spells itself over the phone, and it still fits if you add invoicing
or tax later.
*Weakness:* descriptive rather than distinctive, so harder to trademark than a coined word.
*Searchability:* **ownable**. Nothing else claims the phrase.
`.com` free · `.app` free

**Granary**
The best single word here. A granary is where you keep the surplus so the lean months do not
hurt. Warm, concrete, draws a picture. Sounds like an established company rather than a 2026
app.
*Weakness:* the link to freelance income needs one line of explanation. The domain has been
held since 1994 and is a purchase, not a registration.
*Searchability:* **crowded**. You would share the term with actual granaries, a Leeds
waterfront district and a restaurant chain.
`.com` for sale on Efty · price not visible from RDAP

**Lean Months**
Names the problem your user already feels. Good for marketing copy.
*Weakness:* it names the pain, not the relief. The app would remind people of the bad thing
every time they opened it. Even Months says the same idea from the other side.
*Searchability:* **contested**. "Lean" pulls in lean startup and lean manufacturing.
`.com` free

**The Lay-By**
"Laying by" is the old phrase for setting money aside. Warm and unlike anything in fintech.
*Weakness:* in British English a lay-by is a roadside pull-in, which is what most UK users
will think of first. In the US it reads as "layaway" or as nothing. A name that means
different things in your two biggest markets is a real cost.
`.com` free

**Buffer Month**
Plain and clear. A buffer month is exactly what the product builds.
*Weakness:* **Buffer is an established SaaS brand** selling to the same freelancers and
creators. Different category, same customer. That is where trademark disputes start. Do not
spend money on this without an attorney's opinion.
`.com` free, and the domain is the least of your problems

**Harvest Month**
The metaphor lands and the domain is free.
*Weakness:* **Harvest is a well-known time-tracking product for freelancers.** Same audience,
adjacent category, and your name contains theirs. Cut it.
*Searchability:* **crowded** on top of that.
`.com` free, and it does not matter

**Between Checks**
Describes the moment of pain: the gap between one client paying and the next.
*Weakness:* "checks" is US-specific and dated, since freelancers get paid by transfer. It also
sounds like it could be about medical checkups.
`.com` free

## Top 3

1. **Even Months.** The one I would launch. Names the outcome, needs no explanation, `.com`
   and `.app` both free, misspellings all free. It is the least distinctive of the three and
   that is the price.
2. **Granary.** The better brand if you buy, and the more expensive one twice over. You pay
   Efty for the domain and pay again in content to outrank actual granaries. Get the price
   before you fall in love with it.
3. **Lean Months.** Only if you want the product to lead with the problem.

**Boldest bet:** The Lay-By. Nothing in personal finance sounds like it. It costs you clarity
in the US.

**Safest bet:** Even Months. Free on `.com` and `.app`, no brand collision found, and nothing
about it locks you into budgeting.

## Defensive sweep

Run on Even Months only, since it has the free `.com`.

```
evenmonths.com   free   ← register this
evemonths.com    free   ← the likely typo, $12
```

Everything else in the sweep is free too, including `eevenmonths`. Buy the first two. Nobody
types the rest.

## Check these yourself

- **No trademark search was run, and it matters here.** You are commercial, consumer-facing
  and in financial services, which is policed. "Even Months" is descriptive, so it is cheaper
  to clear and harder to own. Get an attorney search before you spend on the brand.
- **The Buffer and Harvest collisions came from recognising the names, not from a search API.**
  There may be others I did not recognise.
- **App stores were not checked.** Both stores enforce name uniqueness at listing. Search them
  for your final two before you build the listing.
- **Social handles were not checked.** Grab `@evenmonths` everywhere in one sitting.
- **Searchability grades came from reading page one**, not from a ranking tool. They are a
  judgment about how contested a term is.
