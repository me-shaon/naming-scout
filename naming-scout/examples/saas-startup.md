# Example: naming a startup

The common case. A founder with a product and no name. No package registries are checked.
Trademark, app stores and search do the work instead.

Also shows two candidates knocked out by collisions with existing brands, which is the
failure a domain check alone would never catch.

*Checks run 2026-09-09. Re-run before deciding.*

---

**User:** I'm building a budgeting app for freelancers. The problem is irregular income. You
make £9k one month and £900 the next, so normal budgeting apps are useless. We smooth it out
and tell you what you can safely pay yourself each month. Need a name.

**Scout:** Good brief. Three questions:

1. Who says the name out loud, and where? A freelancer recommending it to another
   freelancer, or you pitching investors? It changes how much the name can rely on being
   clever.
2. Does it have to be `.com`? Would you buy one already registered if the price were
   reasonable?
3. Consumer app, so I would check the domain, trademark, both app stores, social handles and
   search presence. No GitHub or npm, since nobody installs this from a terminal. Sound right?

**User:** Freelancers telling other freelancers, mostly. `.com` ideally, and yes I'd pay a
few thousand for the right one. Checks sound right.

**Scout:** Domain mode D then. I will rank on the name and show you what each one costs.

## Territories

The product turns a jagged income line into a flat one. Two ways to name that: the feeling
of steadiness, or the act of storing surplus for later.

- **Steadiness at sea.** Ballast, even keel, trim, slack water. The feeling you are selling.
- **Storing the harvest.** Granary, winter store, lean months, the layby. Older and more
  human. It is what freelancers already do without a name for it.
- **The float.** Money kept aside to cover the gap. Freelancers already use this word.
- **Runway and altitude.** Glide path, reserve line, fuel gauge. Startup-native.
- **Naming the months.** The product's unit is a month. Fat months, thin months, the month
  ahead.

## Round 1: steadiness at sea

```
.com   0 / 12 free
.app   2 / 12 free
```

```
evenkeel.com      registered 1997     ballast.com        registered 1995
trimtab.com       registered 1996     levelset.com       registered 1998
steadyhand.com    registered 2003     counterweight.com  registered 2002
slackwater.com    registered 2004     smoothwater.com    registered 1997
neaptide.com      for_sale   2012     NameBright
```

Zero on `.com`. Every one of these is a real English compound, and they all went between
1995 and 2004. "Even Keel" is the obvious best name in the territory and it has been held
for nearly thirty years.

A permutation tool would now offer `evenkeelapp`, `getevenkeel`, `evenkeelhq`. That is the
wrong move. The territory is mined out, so change territory.

## Round 2: storing the harvest, and the float

```
9 / 18 free on .com   (50%)
```

```
evenmonths.com     free      leanmonths.com      free
harvestmonth.com   free      buffermonth.com     free
thelayby.com       free      keepsomeback.com    free
betweenchecks.com  free      thelongwinter.app   free
granary.com        for_sale   1994    Efty listing
reserveline.com    parked     1999    ParkingCrew
```

From 0% to 50% by changing the metaphor, not the words. Two reasons this territory is open.

**The phrases are longer.** No registrar tool generates `keepsomeback`. Everyone searches
single words and two-word compounds.

**The concept is older than software.** Storing grain for winter is not a fintech metaphor,
so fintech has not strip-mined it.

## Round 3: refining on the surviving stems

```
7 / 13 free on .com
```

`flatmonths`, `smoothmonth`, `thinmonths`, `fatmonths`, `thegoodmonths`, `everymonthsame`
and `monthfloat` are all free. `levelpay.com` is listed on Dan. `themonthahead.com` has been
parked since 2020.

None of them beat `evenmonths`. Stopping here.

## Shortlist

**Even Months** (Naming the months)
It states the outcome in two words. Irregular income made even. A freelancer understands it
before you finish the sentence, with no tagline and no explanation. It spells itself over the
phone. It survives if you later add invoicing or tax, because the promise is about the month,
not about budgeting.
*Weakness:* it is descriptive rather than distinctive, so it will be harder to own in
trademark than a coined word.
*Searchability:* **ownable**. Nothing else claims the exact phrase, so you would be the first
result within weeks of launch.
`.com` free · `.app` free · full defensive sweep free

**Granary** (Storing the harvest)
The best single word in the run. A granary is where you keep the surplus so the lean months
do not hurt. One word, warm, concrete, and it draws a picture. It sounds like an established
company rather than a 2026 app.
*Weakness:* the connection to freelance income needs one line of explanation the first time.
The domain has been held since 1994 and is listed on Efty, so this is a purchase, not a
registration. Price is not visible from RDAP.
*Searchability:* **crowded**. You would share the term with actual granaries, a Leeds
waterfront district and a chain of restaurants. Add the aftermarket price to the cost of
ranking for your own name.
`.com` for_sale, Efty listing, held since 1994

**Lean Months** (Storing the harvest)
Names the problem your user already feels. Strong for marketing copy, and instantly
recognised by anyone who has had a bad quarter.
*Weakness:* it names the pain rather than the relief. A product called Lean Months reminds
the user of the bad thing every time they open it. Even Months names the same idea from the
other side.
*Searchability:* **contested**. "Lean" pulls in lean startup and lean manufacturing results.
`.com` free

**The Lay-By** (Storing the harvest)
"Laying by" is the old phrase for setting money aside for later. It is warm, slightly
old-fashioned, and unlike anything else in fintech.
*Weakness:* in British English a lay-by is also a roadside pull-in, which is the first
meaning most UK users will reach for. In the US the phrase reads as "layaway" or as nothing
at all. A name that means different things in your two biggest markets is a real cost.
`.com` free

**Buffer Month** (The float)
A buffer month is exactly what the product builds. Plain, functional, immediately clear.
*Weakness:* **Buffer is an established SaaS brand** with a large freelance and creator
audience. That is the same buyer you are selling to. Different category, same customer, and
that is where trademark disputes actually happen. I would not spend money on this without an
attorney's opinion first.
`.com` free, and the domain is the least of your problems here

**Harvest Month** (Storing the harvest)
The metaphor lands and the domain is free.
*Weakness:* **Harvest is a well-known time-tracking and invoicing product for freelancers.**
Same audience, adjacent category, and your name contains theirs. This is the strongest
collision in the run. Cut it.
*Searchability:* **crowded**, on top of everything else. You would be competing with an
established product and with the agricultural sense of the word.
`.com` free, and it does not matter

**Between Checks** (Naming the months)
Describes the actual moment of pain: the gap between one client paying and the next.
*Weakness:* "checks" is US-specific and already dated, since freelancers are paid by
transfer. It also sounds like it might be about medical checkups.
`.com` free

**Reserve Line** (Runway and altitude)
A reserve line is a credit facility you do not draw on. Precise and financial.
*Weakness:* it sounds like a bank product rather than an app for one person, and the tone is
corporate where the audience is not. Parked since 1999, so there is no listing and no price.
`.com` parked since 1999, no listing found

## Top 3

1. **Even Months.** The one I would launch. It names the outcome, needs no explanation, the
   `.com` and `.app` are both free, and the whole defensive sweep is open. It is the least
   distinctive of the three and that is the price.
2. **Granary.** The better brand if you are willing to buy, and the more expensive one twice
   over. You pay Efty for the domain and then you pay again in content and links to outrank
   actual granaries for your own name. Get the price before you fall in love with it.
3. **Lean Months.** The marketing-led choice. Take it only if you want the product to lead
   with the problem.

**Boldest bet:** The Lay-By. Nothing in personal finance sounds like it, which is the whole
argument for it. It costs you clarity in the US market.

**Safest bet:** Even Months. Free on `.com` and `.app`, no brand collision found, the
misspellings are all free, and nothing about it locks you into budgeting specifically.

## Defensive sweep on the leader

Run on Even Months only, because it is the one with a free `.com`:

```
evenmonths.com   free   ← register this
evemonths.com    free   ← dropped 'n', the likely typo. Worth $12.
evenmonth.com    checked separately
```

Every other variant in the sweep is free too, including `eevenmonths` and `evenmnoths`.
Buy the first two. Nobody types the rest.

## Check these yourself

- **Trademark has not been searched and it matters here.** You are commercial, consumer-facing
  and in financial services, which is a policed area. "Even Months" is descriptive, which
  makes it cheaper to clear and harder to own. Get an attorney search before you spend on the
  brand. The strongest thing I could ever tell you is "no obvious conflict in the marks I
  searched". That is not clearance.
- **The Buffer and Harvest collisions above came from recognising the names, not from a
  search API.** There may be others I did not recognise. A professional search is the only
  way to know.
- **App stores were not checked.** Both stores enforce name uniqueness at listing time. Search
  the App Store and Google Play for your final two before you build the listing.
- **Social handles were not checked.** Status codes are wrong in both directions often enough
  to be useless. `@evenmonths` is worth grabbing on every platform in one sitting.
- **Searchability grades came from reading page one, not from a ranking tool.** They are a
  judgment about how contested a term is, not a measured difficulty score.
- `granary.com` is listed, which tells you a price exists. It does not tell you the price.
  Open the Efty listing.
