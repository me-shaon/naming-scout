# Example: consumer product

Shows: no technical registry checks at all, trademark as the dominant risk, and an honest
report when a territory does not recover.

*Checks run 2026-09-08. Re-run before deciding.*

---

**User:** Refillable, plastic-free dish soap. Aluminium bottle, refill tablets. The point is
that it looks good enough to leave on the counter instead of hiding under the sink. Premium
but warm, not clinical. Selling DTC in the US and UK.

**Scout:** Physical product sold commercially, so the risk profile is different from a
software name. Trademark matters more here than the domain does — you can build on a
`.co` or a compound `.com`, but you cannot build on a name someone else owns in class 3.

Checks: domain, trademark search (US and UK, class 3 for cleaning preparations), social
handles at low confidence, and a search pass for existing brands. No GitHub, no npm, no
app stores. Two questions:

1. Retail eventually, or DTC only? Retail means the name has to work on a shelf next to
   Method and Blueland, and it means a hard trademark requirement.
2. Is a two-word `.com` acceptable, or does the brand need a single word?

**User:** Retail eventually, yes. Two words is fine if it's good.

## Territories

- **Water states** — still, spring, rain, brine, tide. Warm, natural, premium-adjacent.
- **Domestic ritual** — scullery, washhouse, basin, the daily rinse. Nostalgic, tactile,
  fits "leave it on the counter".
- **Ceramics and glaze** — delftware, saltglaze, glazeware. Unexpected for soap, and it
  points at the object rather than the chemistry, which is the actual differentiator.
- **Plain-speaking** — plain soap, honest suds. Fits the anti-greenwashing angle, but this
  is the most crowded language in the whole category.
- **Coined/abstract** — the route most successful brands here took (Blueland, Dropps,
  Cleancult). Costs marketing spend, buys a clean trademark.

11% is a small recovery and not enough. Both survivors are weak: `softbasin` is a phrase
nobody says, and `sudsmith` is a portmanteau that fails the phone test (`suds-smith` or
`sud-smith`?).

**This is where I stop pretending the round worked.** Two available domains attached to two
mediocre names is not a shortlist. The honest read of these two rounds is that the entire
real-word territory is closed on `.com` for this category, and the choice is between three
routes, not between these names.

## The three real routes

**Route 1 — buy from the aftermarket.** The best names in both rounds are listed, not free.
`glazeware.com` has been held since 1999 and is on Afternic; `brinehouse.com` was registered
in 2025 and listed immediately, which is inventory rather than a business. Neither price is
visible from RDAP. For a retail-bound consumer brand this is usually the correct spend: the
domain is a one-off cost against a brand you will spend far more on.

**Route 2 — coin a word.** Blueland, Dropps, Cleancult and Cleanomic all did this, and the
reason is visible in these two rounds: real words are gone and coined words are trademark-
clean. It costs you meaning that you then have to buy back with marketing.

**Route 3 — a compound `.com` with a modifier.** `thescullery.com`, `sculleryco.com`. Cheap,
available, and it permanently signals that someone else has the real name.

I would take route 1 or route 2. Route 3 undercuts a premium positioning at exactly the
point a customer is deciding whether to trust the price.

## Candidates worth pursuing, by route

**Glazeware** — *Ceramics* — aftermarket
The glaze is what makes a functional object worth looking at, which is the entire product
thesis in one word. Warm, tactile, and it points at the bottle rather than the chemistry.
*Weakness:* reads as a homewares or ceramics brand before it reads as soap, so the category
has to be carried by the packaging and the copy. And "-ware" suggests a product range,
which is a commitment if you only ever sell one thing.
`.com` for_sale, Afternic, held since 1999 · price not visible from RDAP

**Brinehouse** — *Water states* — aftermarket
Brine is salt water: preserving, elemental, faintly coastal. "House" gives it a maker's
identity rather than a product name.
*Weakness:* brine also means pickling, and for a product you put on things you eat off,
the food association is not entirely helpful. Registered 2025 and listed immediately, so
expect speculative pricing on a name with no history.
`.com` for_sale, Atom listing

**Scullery** — *Domestic ritual* — approach the holder
The best name of the run and the one I would want. A scullery is the small back room where
washing-up was done: exactly the humble task this brand is elevating, and the word is warm,
old and unmistakably domestic.
*Weakness:* British-leaning; a US shopper may not know the word, which is a real cost when
half your market is US retail. And `scullery.com` has been held since 2000 by a live
registrant, so this is an approach rather than a purchase.
`.com` registered 2000 · alternates worth checking: `.co`, `scullerysoap.com`

**Softbasin** · **Sudsmith** — available, and I do not recommend either. Listed so you can
see what "available" produced in this category rather than taking my word for it.

## What I would do

Run one more round in the coined territory before committing, and in parallel get prices on
`glazeware.com` and `scullery.com`. If glazeware comes in under a few thousand it is the
best value in the run: a name with meaning, in a category where everyone else had to invent
one.

## Not verified

- **No trademark search has been run yet.** For a retail-bound cleaning product this is the
  check that matters most, and it needs to happen before you spend anything on the domain.
  Class 3 (cleaning preparations) is crowded, and consumer goods is the area where large
  companies actively police marks. When it is run, the strongest thing I will be able to
  say is "no obvious conflict in the marks I searched" — that is not clearance. Get an
  attorney search before packaging goes to print.
- Aftermarket prices are not in RDAP. The `for_sale` state means a listing exists, not that
  it is affordable. Open the listings.
- Social handles not checked; see `references/clearance-guide.md` for why not.
- UK and US search passes done on the names alone. Neither covers non-indexed local brands,
  which are common in home goods.
