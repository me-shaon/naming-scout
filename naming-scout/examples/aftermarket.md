# Example: the user will buy the domain

Domain mode D. All five states side by side. A registered name stays at the top of the ranking
because the user said they would pay.

*Checks run 2026-09-08. Re-run before deciding.*

---

**User:** B2B SaaS. Sales-tax compliance for e-commerce sellers. We watch your sales by state,
tell you when you cross an economic nexus threshold, and file for you. Funded, so we can spend
on the domain. Serious and trustworthy. This is money and audits.

I want the best name. I don't care if it's registered, I'll buy it.

**Scout:** That changes the run. I will rank on the name and show what each one costs. Every
line carries its state, so you always know what you are looking at.

One question. Is there a number above which you walk away?

**User:** Under $15k, yes. Above $30k, no.

**Scout:** Checks: domain, trademark, GitHub org, search, LinkedIn. Regulated-adjacent and
funded, so trademark is the check with real consequences. No package registries, no app stores.

## Territories

- **Threshold and boundary.** Economic nexus is a threshold you cross. The product tells you
  where the line is.
- **Safe harbour.** A real tax-law term and a metaphor for the feeling you are selling. That
  overlap is rare.
- **State lines.** The problem is entirely about crossing borders.
- **Ledger and tally.** Conventional for the category, which is both a cost and a comfort.
- **Wake and trail.** What your sales leave behind. Riskier.


Each state is a different answer.

**`available`** is a 404 at the registry. Registration price, today, no negotiation.

**`for_sale`** means the nameservers point at a marketplace, so the holder is advertising. The
price is on the listing, not in the DNS. Rough guidance: HugeDomains sits in the low four
figures and rarely negotiates. Atom, formerly Squadhelp, usually lists between $2k and $25k.
Afternic and Sedo span everything. All three land inside your band often enough to open.

**`parked`** means a monetisation host with no listing. `harborline.com` has been parked since
2000, which usually means ad revenue rather than an asking price. You can email the holder.
Reply rates are low and they set the anchor. **This is registered, not available.** A registrar
search box would mislead you, and so would checking whether a website loads.

**`registered`** means somebody holds it and is not advertising. `safeharbor.com` since 1995 is
a live asset. Treat it as unobtainable.

The four names registered between 2024 and 2026 are their own signal. Other people are naming
in this space right now, along the same lines. That is a distinctiveness warning, not a domain
problem.

## Shortlist

**Nexusmark** (`for_sale` on Atom, held since 2014)
"Nexus" is the exact legal term for the connection that triggers a filing obligation. Precise
to a buyer who knows the category, neutral to one who does not. "Mark" adds a line being drawn
and a record being kept. It sounds like infrastructure, right for something that files on your
behalf.
*Weakness:* "nexus" is heavily used across B2B software, so the name is precise inside tax and
generic outside it. Atom is a curated marketplace, so the price reflects brand value rather
than registration cost.
Trademark needs a real search. "Nexus" marks are numerous. GitHub `nexusmark` is free.

**Safe Harbor** (`.com` registered 1995, unobtainable)
The strongest name in the run, and you cannot have the `.com`. In tax law a safe harbour is a
provision that protects you if you meet its conditions. That is what the product sells. As an
image it carries the emotional promise too. Two meanings, both correct, no explanation needed.
*Weakness:* a common term of art used by hundreds of financial companies, so it is very hard to
own in trademark and very hard to rank for. Realistic routes are `safeharbor.tax`,
`getsafeharbor.com`, or dropping it.
Listed because you asked for the best name. Pretending otherwise would be dishonest ranking.

**Nexusline** (`for_sale` on HugeDomains, held since 2012)
The line you cross. Cleaner and more concrete than Nexusmark.
*Weakness:* "-line" is a tired B2B suffix, and there are four other `-line` names in this
shortlist, which tells you how reachable that construction is. HugeDomains listings are usually
firm-priced.

**Taxseam** (`available`)
A seam is where two things meet and where things leak. Registration price, no negotiation.
*Weakness:* "seam" also suggests a flaw. "Tax" in the name is a permanent category lock, so
expanding into broader compliance would mean a rebrand. It also sounds like a filing utility
rather than a platform.

**Dueline** (`for_sale` on Atom, held since 2024)
"Due" carries the deadline and the amount owed. Short and plain.
*Weakness:* one letter from "dueling" and a homophone for "duly". Registered in 2024 and listed
at once, so it is speculative inventory rather than an aged asset.

**Taxwake** (`available`)
The wake your sales leave across state lines. Genuinely unusual for this category.
*Weakness:* "wake" reads as funeral first for a large share of English speakers. Wrong
association for a compliance product. Fails the tone requirement.

## Top 3

1. **Nexusmark.** Open the Atom listing today. The only name that is precise inside the
   category, credible outside it, and actually purchasable. Get a trademark search before you
   make an offer, not after.
2. **Nexusline.** The fallback if Nexusmark prices above your band.
3. **Taxseam.** The free option. Take it if you would rather put the budget into the product,
   and accept the category lock.

**Boldest bet:** none recommended. Taxwake is the only unusual name here and its central
association is wrong. Saying so is more useful than promoting a safe name into the slot.

**Safest bet:** Taxseam. Available now, no negotiation, no aftermarket exposure. The least
interesting name here, and it takes the domain off your critical path.

## Check these yourself

- **No listing prices were retrieved.** RDAP has no price field. `for_sale` means a listing
  exists. The marketplace bands above are guidance, not quotes. Open the listings.
- `harborline.com` is parked with no listing found. No price, and no evidence the holder wants
  to sell.
- **Trademark has not been searched, and it matters most for you.** Funded, commercial,
  compliance-adjacent, with "nexus" as a candidate root. That needs an attorney's clearance
  search. The strongest thing I can ever tell you is "no obvious conflict in the marks I
  searched".
- "Safeharbor" and "nexus" both return heavy generic results, so SEO on either will be a fight
  regardless of who holds the domain.
