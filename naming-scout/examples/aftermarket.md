**User:** Under $15k, yes. Above $30k, no.

**Scout:** Then I will surface everything and flag which marketplaces tend to price in which

Checks: domain, trademark, GitHub org, search presence, LinkedIn. This is a regulated-adjacent

registries, no app stores.

## Territories

- **Threshold and boundary.** Economic nexus is a threshold you cross. The product's job is
  telling you where the line is.
- **Safe harbour.** "Safe harbor" is a real tax-law term and a metaphor for the feeling you
  are selling. That overlap is rare. Worth mining hard.
- **State lines and geography.** The compliance problem is entirely about crossing borders.
- **Ledger and tally.** The accounting register. Conventional for the category, which is both
  a cost and a comfort.
- **Wake and trail.** What your sales leave behind. Less obvious, riskier.

Each state is a different answer, so read them separately.

**`available`** means a 404 at the registry. Registration price, today, no negotiation.

**`for_sale`** means the nameservers point at a marketplace. The holder is advertising. The
price sits on the listing, not in the DNS. As rough guidance: HugeDomains prices in the low
four figures and rarely negotiates. Atom, formerly Squadhelp, is a curated brandable
marketplace and usually lists between $2k and $25k. Afternic and Sedo span everything. All
three land inside your band often enough to be worth opening.

**`parked`** means the nameservers point at a monetisation host with no marketplace listing.
`harborline.com` has been parked since 2000, which usually means ad revenue rather than an
asking price. You can email the holder. Reply rates are low and the holder sets the anchor.
**This is registered, not available.** A registrar search box would mislead you about it. So
would checking whether a website loads.

**`registered`** means somebody holds it and is not advertising. `safeharbor.com` since 1995
is a live asset, not inventory. Treat it as unobtainable.

The four names registered in 2024 to 2026 are their own signal. `thresholdtax`, `tallyline`,
`filingline` and `bracketline` all went recently. Other people are naming in this space right
now, along the same lines. That is a distinctiveness warning, not a domain problem.

## Shortlist

Ranked on the name. The domain is a cost line. You asked for this ranking when you said you
would buy.

**Nexusmark** (Threshold. `for_sale` on Atom, held since 2014)
"Nexus" is the exact legal term for the connection that triggers a filing obligation. The
name is precise to a buyer who knows the category and neutral to one who does not. "Mark"
adds a line being drawn and a record being kept. It sounds like infrastructure, which is
right for something that files on your behalf.
*Weakness:* "nexus" is heavily used across B2B software, so the name is precise inside tax
and generic outside it. Atom is a curated marketplace, so the price reflects brand value
rather than registration cost.
Trademark needs a real search. "Nexus" marks are numerous. GitHub `nexusmark` is free.

**Safe Harbor** (Safe harbour. `.com` registered 1995, unobtainable)
The strongest name in the run, and you cannot have the `.com`. In tax law a safe harbour is
a provision that protects you if you meet its conditions. That is what the product sells. As
an image it carries the emotional promise too. Two meanings, both correct, no explanation
needed.
*Weakness:* it is a common term of art used by hundreds of financial companies, so it is very
hard to own in trademark and very hard to rank for. `safeharbor.com` has been held since 1995
by a live holder. Realistic routes are `safeharbor.tax`, `getsafeharbor.com`, or dropping it.
It is listed here because you asked for the best name. Pretending it is not would be
dishonest ranking.

**Nexusline** (Threshold. `for_sale` on HugeDomains, held since 2012)
The line you cross. Cleaner and more concrete than Nexusmark. It states the product's core
function in one image.
*Weakness:* "-line" is a tired B2B suffix. There are four other `-line` names in this
shortlist, which tells you how reachable that construction is. HugeDomains listings are
usually firm-priced.

**Taxseam** (Threshold. `available`)
A seam is where two things meet and where things leak. Available at registration price, so
zero domain cost and no negotiation.
*Weakness:* "seam" also suggests a flaw or a split. "Tax" in the name is a permanent category
lock, so expanding into broader compliance would mean a rebrand. Leading with the tax word
also makes it sound like a filing utility rather than a platform.

**Dueline** (Ledger. `for_sale` on Atom, held since 2024)
"Due" carries both the deadline and the amount owed. Short, plain, easy to say.
*Weakness:* one letter from "dueling" and a homophone for "duly". Registered in 2024 and
listed at once, so it is speculative inventory rather than an aged asset. Worth knowing
before you open a negotiation.

**Taxwake** (Wake and trail. `available`)
The wake your sales leave across state lines. Genuinely unusual for this category.
*Weakness:* "wake" reads as funeral first for a large share of English speakers. That is the
wrong association for a compliance product. It fails the tone requirement.

## Top 3

1. **Nexusmark.** Open the Atom listing today. It is the only name that is precise inside the
   category, credible outside it, and actually purchasable. Get a trademark search before you
   make an offer, not after.
2. **Nexusline.** The fallback if Nexusmark prices above your band. Slightly more generic,
   slightly clearer.
3. **Taxseam.** The free option. Take it if you would rather put the domain budget into the
   product. Accept the category lock.

**Boldest bet:** none worth recommending. Taxwake is the only genuinely unusual name here and
its central association is wrong for the product. Saying so is more useful than promoting a
safe name into the slot.

**Safest bet:** Taxseam. Available now, no negotiation, no aftermarket exposure. It is the
least interesting name here and it takes the domain off your critical path entirely.

## Check these yourself

- **No listing prices were retrieved.** RDAP has no price field. `for_sale` means a listing
  exists. The marketplace bands above are general guidance, not quotes. Open the listings.
- `harborline.com` is parked with no marketplace listing found. There is no price and no
  evidence the holder wants to sell. Any plan that depends on it is speculative.
- **Trademark has not been searched. It is the check that matters most for you.** Funded,
  commercial, compliance-adjacent, with "nexus" as a candidate root. That combination needs an
  attorney's clearance search, not mine. The strongest thing I will ever be able to tell you
  is "no obvious conflict in the marks I searched".
- Search presence was checked on exact terms only. "Safeharbor" and "nexus" both return heavy
  generic results, so SEO on either will be a fight regardless of who holds the domain.
