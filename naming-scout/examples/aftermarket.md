**Scout:** Then I will surface everything and flag which listings tend to price in which

## Territories

- **Threshold and boundary** — economic nexus is literally a threshold you cross. The
  product's job is telling you where the line is.
- **Maritime safe harbour** — "safe harbor" is a genuine tax-law term *and* a metaphor for
  the feeling you are selling. Rare overlap; worth mining hard.
- **State lines and geography** — the compliance problem is entirely about crossing borders.
- **Ledger and tally** — the accounting register. Conventional for the category, which is a
  cost and a comfort.
- **Wake and trail** — what your sales leave behind. Less obvious, riskier.

Reading the states, because they are all different answers:

- **`available`** — 404 at the registry. Registration price, today, no negotiation.
- **`for_sale`** — nameservers point at a marketplace. The holder is advertising. Price is
  on the listing, not in the DNS. As a rough guide: HugeDomains prices in the low
  four figures and rarely negotiates; Atom (formerly Squadhelp) is a curated brandable
  marketplace and typically lists in the $2k–$25k band; Afternic and Sedo span everything.
  All three are inside your range often enough to be worth opening.
- **`parked`** — nameservers point at a monetisation host with no marketplace listing.
  `harborline.com` has been parked since 2000, which usually means ad revenue rather than
  an asking price. Approachable by email, low reply rate, and the holder sets the anchor.
  **This is registered, not available.** A registrar search box and a "no website here"
  check would both mislead you about it.
- **`registered`** — someone holds it and is not advertising. `safeharbor.com` since 1995 is
  a live asset, not inventory; treat it as unobtainable and move on.
- The four names registered in 2024–2026 (`thresholdtax`, `tallyline`, `filingline`,
  `bracketline`) are a signal in themselves: other people are naming in this space right
  now, along the same lines. That is a distinctiveness warning, not a domain problem.

## Shortlist

Ranked on the name, with the domain as a cost line. This is the ranking the user asked for
by saying they would buy.

**Nexusmark** — *Threshold* — `for_sale`, Atom, since 2014
"Nexus" is the exact legal term for the connection that triggers a filing obligation, so
the name is precise to a buyer who knows the category and neutral to one who does not.
"Mark" adds the sense of a line being drawn and of a record being kept. It sounds like
infrastructure, which is right for something that files on your behalf.
*Weakness:* "nexus" is heavily used in B2B software generally, so it is precise inside tax
and generic outside it. And Atom is a curated marketplace, which means the price reflects
brand value, not registration cost.
Trademark: needs a real search — "nexus" marks are numerous · GitHub `nexusmark` free

**Safe Harbor** — *Maritime / tax term* — `.com` registered 1995, unobtainable
The strongest name in the run and you cannot have the `.com`. In tax law, a safe harbour is
a provision that protects you if you meet its conditions — which is exactly what the
product sells — and as an image it carries the emotional promise too. Two meanings, both
correct, no explanation needed.
*Weakness:* it is a common-law term of art used by hundreds of financial companies, which
makes it very hard to own in trademark and very hard to rank for. `safeharbor.com` has been
held since 1995 by a live holder. Realistic routes are `safeharbor.tax`, `getsafeharbor.com`,
or dropping it.
Listed here because you asked for the best name, and pretending it is not the best name
would be dishonest ranking.

**Nexusline** — *Threshold* — `for_sale`, HugeDomains, since 2012
The line you cross. Cleaner and more concrete than Nexusmark, and it says the product's
core function in one image.
*Weakness:* "-line" is a tired B2B suffix, and there are four other `-line` names in this
very shortlist, which tells you how reachable that construction is. HugeDomains listings
are typically firm-priced and rarely negotiable.

**Taxseam** — *Threshold* — `available`
A seam is where two things meet and where things leak. Available at registration price, so
zero domain cost and no negotiation.
*Weakness:* "seam" also suggests a flaw or a split, and "tax" in the name is a permanent
category lock — if you expand into broader compliance it will need a rebrand. Leading with
the tax word also makes it sound like a filing utility rather than a platform.

**Dueline** — *Ledger* — `for_sale`, Atom, since 2024
"Due" carries the deadline and the amount owed. Short, plain, easy to say.
*Weakness:* one letter from "dueling" and homophonic with "duly". Registered in 2024 and
listed immediately, which means speculative inventory rather than an aged asset — worth
knowing before you open a negotiation.

**Taxwake** — *Wake and trail* — `available`
The wake your sales leave across state lines. Genuinely unusual for this category.
*Weakness:* "wake" reads as funeral first for a substantial share of English speakers, which
is not the association you want on a compliance product. Fails the tone requirement.

## Top 3

1. **Nexusmark** — I would open the Atom listing today. It is the only name that is precise
   inside the category, credible outside it, and actually purchasable. Get a trademark
   search on it before you make an offer, not after.
2. **Nexusline** — the fallback if Nexusmark prices above your band. Slightly more generic,
   slightly clearer.
3. **Taxseam** — the free option. Take it if you would rather put the domain budget into
   the product, and accept the category lock.

**Best unconventional:** none in this run worth recommending. Taxwake is the only genuinely
unusual name and its central association is wrong for the product. Saying so is more useful
than promoting a safe name into the slot.

**Safest:** Taxseam. Available now, no negotiation, no aftermarket exposure. Least
interesting name here, and it removes the domain from your critical path entirely.

  exists; the marketplace bands above are general guidance, not quotes. Open the listings.
- `harborline.com` is parked with no marketplace listing found, so there is no price and no
  evidence the holder wants to sell. Treat any plan that depends on it as speculative.
- **Trademark: not yet searched, and this is the check that matters most for you.** Funded,
  commercial, in a compliance-adjacent category, with "nexus" as a candidate root — that
  combination needs an attorney's clearance search, not mine. The strongest thing I will
  ever be able to tell you is "no obvious conflict in the marks I searched".
- Search presence checked on exact terms only. `safeharbor` and `nexus` both return heavy
  generic results, so SEO on either will be a fight regardless of who holds the domain.
