# Example: newsletter

Shows: domain + social + search only (no package registries), format lock as a naming
constraint, and the availability rate recovering sharply when the territory changes.

*Checks run 2026-09-08. Re-run before deciding.*

---

**User:** Weekly newsletter about how independent musicians actually make money. Touring,
merch, sync licensing, streaming, the real numbers. Audience is working musicians, not
industry people. Practical and a bit irreverent.

**Scout:** Two questions and I will generate:

1. Do you want the name to survive if this becomes a podcast or a course later? That rules
   out anything with "weekly", "letter", or "issue" in it.
2. Handles: are you willing to run `@nameHQ` or `@getname` if the bare handle is gone, or
   does the exact handle matter?

**User:** Yes, keep it open — I'll probably do a podcast. Handles, I'd rather have the
bare one but I won't die on it.

**Scout:** Profile: domain (`.com`, since a newsletter gets typed from memory), search
presence, and the social handles at low confidence. No GitHub, no npm, no PyPI, no app
stores, no trademark unless this becomes a business.

## Territories

- **Insider vocabulary** — the words musicians use with each other and nobody else uses:
  door split, backline, load-in, per diem, the guarantee, merch table, split sheet. Highest
  signal per word in the whole run, and only someone in the scene can supply it.
- **The arithmetic** — this newsletter is really about numbers. Break-even, the cut, the
  gate, the math of a tour.
- **Imperatives and phrases** — a title that says something rather than labels a topic.
- **Reference works** — handbook, field guide, ledger. Fits the teaching half.
- **Road and touring** — the van, the route, the overnight drive.

5% is a dead territory on `.com`. The words are perfect and every one of them was taken
twenty years ago. This is the point where a permutation tool would start producing
`merchtable-hq`, `themerchtable`, `merchtabledaily`, and the run would quietly become bad.

The right move is a different territory, not a worse version of this one.

```
roadmoney.com        for_sale   2019    Afternic
```

Nearly a tenfold recovery. Two reasons, and both are reusable:

**Phrases are unmined.** No registrar tool generates `whatthegigpays`. The permutation
space everyone searches is single words and two-word compounds, so multi-word phrases are
almost untouched. This matches the source research, where imperative and phrase clusters
ran at 45% while umbrella nouns ran at 13%.

**The `-math` compounds are all taken and all recent** — 2015, 2024, 2024, 2025. That is a
pattern worth reading: a naming formula that suddenly looks obvious to everyone at once
gets exhausted in a couple of years. `bandmath` being registered in 2024 tells you other
people had this exact idea recently.

## Shortlist

**The Door Split** — *Insider vocabulary + arithmetic*
The door split is how a band gets paid at a small venue: a percentage of ticket money after
the venue takes its cut. It is the single most concrete image of "how musicians actually
make money", and every working musician knows it while almost no one outside does. That
in-group signal is exactly the positioning.
*Weakness:* it names one revenue line. The newsletter also covers sync, streaming and merch,
so there is a mild mismatch between the title's specificity and the scope. Survivable —
readers will take it as a synecdoche — but it is a real cost.
`.com` available · handles: `@thedoorsplit` probably free, `@doorsplit` likely gone

**What the Gig Pays** — *Imperative/phrase*
Says the whole proposition in four words with no tagline needed. Works identically as a
podcast title, which was the stated requirement, and it is the kind of name a reader
repeats verbatim when recommending it.
*Weakness:* fifteen characters and four words. Long to type, awkward as a handle, and
"gig" has drifted toward gig-economy meaning in the last decade — some readers will expect
freelance work rather than live music.
`.com` available

**Worth the Gig** — *Phrase*
Shorter, and it carries a judgment rather than a topic: the newsletter's actual job is
helping musicians decide which opportunities are worth taking. Ambiguous in a useful way.
*Weakness:* also ambiguous in a less useful way — it could be read as a review publication
about shows. Needs a subtitle for the first year.
`.com` available

**Count the Gate** — *Arithmetic*
"The gate" is the ticket revenue. "Count the gate" is an instruction to check the number
yourself rather than trust what you were told, which is a strong editorial stance for this
audience.
*Weakness:* the sharpest in-group barrier of the shortlist. A musician five years in gets
it immediately; someone in their first year does not, and that is a meaningful share of the
audience for a newsletter about how the money works.
`.com` available

**Break-Even Tour** — *Arithmetic*
Names the universal experience: the tour that costs what it makes. Instant recognition and
some rueful humour, which fits the irreverent brief.
*Weakness:* it is a downbeat frame for a publication whose promise is that you can do
better than break even. Naming the problem rather than the outcome.
`.com` available

**All In the Gate** · **Cut of the Door** · **Play for Nothing** — same territories, all
available, all weaker. `playfornothing.com` is the funniest and the most likely to be
misread as advocacy for playing free.

## Top 3

1. **The Door Split** — the one I would launch. Concrete, in-group, short enough for a
   handle, no format lock, and it sounds like a publication rather than a topic.
2. **What the Gig Pays** — the clearest and the most likely to be forwarded. Pick it if
   reach matters more than insider credibility, and accept the length.
3. **Worth the Gig** — the best compromise, and the weakest identity. It is what you pick
   if the other two feel too committed.

**Best unconventional:** Count the Gate. Highest ceiling, narrowest door. It would build a
sharper community faster and grow more slowly.

**Safest:** The Door Split. `.com` free, no format lock, no meaningful collision, and it
reads correctly whether it becomes a podcast, a course, or a book.

## Not verified

- **Social handles were not machine-checked.** Platform status codes are unreliable in both
  directions and I would rather send you to the signup pages than give you a confident
  wrong answer. Reserve on all platforms in one sitting; expect the bare handle to be gone
  on the older ones and plan the `@get-` or `@the-` fallback now.
- Search: no established brand found on these exact phrases, but "door split" and "the gate"
  return generic music-industry results, so expect to compete for the term rather than own
  it immediately. Absence of a brand in search is weak evidence — plenty of live
  publications are not indexed on their own name.
- No trademark search. Correct at newsletter stage. Do one before you sell anything under
  the name.
