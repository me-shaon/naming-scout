# Example: newsletter

Shows domain, social and search only, with no package registries. Shows format lock as a
naming constraint. Shows the availability rate recovering sharply when the territory changes.

*Checks run 2026-09-08. Re-run before deciding.*

---

**User:** Weekly newsletter about how independent musicians actually make money. Touring,
merch, sync licensing, streaming, the real numbers. Audience is working musicians, not
industry people. Practical and a bit irreverent.

**Scout:** Two questions and I will generate:

1. Should the name survive if this becomes a podcast or a course later? That rules out
   anything containing "weekly", "letter" or "issue".
2. If the bare handle is gone, will you run `@nameHQ` or `@getname`, or does the exact
   handle matter?

**User:** Yes, keep it open, I'll probably do a podcast. Handles, I'd rather have the bare
one but I won't die on it.

**Scout:** Checks: `.com`, since a newsletter gets typed from memory. Search presence.
Social handles at low confidence. No GitHub, no npm, no PyPI, no app stores. Trademark only
if this becomes a business.

## Territories

- **Insider vocabulary.** Words musicians use with each other and nobody else uses. Door
  split, backline, load-in, per diem, the guarantee, merch table, split sheet. Highest signal
  per word in the run. Only someone in the scene can supply these.
- **The arithmetic.** This newsletter is about numbers. Break-even, the cut, the gate, the
  math of a tour.
- **Imperatives and phrases.** A title that says something instead of labelling a topic.
- **Reference works.** Handbook, field guide, ledger. Fits the teaching half.
- **Road and touring.** The van, the route, the overnight drive.


```
thedoorsplit.com     free      worththegig.com      free
cutofthedoor.com     free      countthegate.com     free

gigmath.com          registered 2024    tourmath.com     registered 2015
roadmoney.com        for_sale   2019    Afternic
```

Nearly a tenfold recovery. Two reasons, both reusable.

**Phrases are unmined.** No registrar tool generates `whatthegigpays`. Everyone searches
single words and two-word compounds, so multi-word phrases sit almost untouched. The source
research found the same thing. Imperative and phrase clusters ran at 45% while umbrella nouns
ran at 13%.

**The `-math` compounds are all taken and all recent.** 2015, 2024, 2024, 2025. Read that as
a warning. A naming formula that suddenly looks obvious to everyone gets exhausted in a
couple of years. `bandmath` going in 2024 means other people had this exact idea recently.

## Shortlist

**The Door Split** (Insider vocabulary and arithmetic)
The door split is how a band gets paid at a small venue: a percentage of ticket money after
the venue takes its cut. It is the most concrete image of how musicians actually make money.
Every working musician knows it. Almost nobody outside does. That in-group signal is the
positioning.
*Weakness:* it names one revenue line. The newsletter also covers sync, streaming and merch.
Readers will take it as shorthand, but the mismatch is real.
`.com` free · `@thedoorsplit` probably free · `@doorsplit` likely gone

**What the Gig Pays** (Phrase)
Four words that state the whole proposition with no tagline. It works identically as a
podcast title, which was the stated requirement. Readers repeat it verbatim when they
recommend it.
*Weakness:* fifteen characters and four words. Slow to type, awkward as a handle. "Gig" has
drifted toward the gig economy over the last decade, so some readers will expect freelance
work rather than live music.
`.com` free

**Worth the Gig** (Phrase)
Shorter, and it carries a judgment instead of a topic. The newsletter's real job is helping
musicians decide which offers to take. The ambiguity works in its favour.
*Weakness:* it is also ambiguous in a way that does not help. It could read as a review
publication about shows. Needs a subtitle for the first year.
`.com` free

**Count the Gate** (Arithmetic)
"The gate" is the ticket revenue. "Count the gate" is an instruction to check the number
yourself instead of trusting what you were told. That is a strong editorial stance for this
audience.
*Weakness:* the sharpest in-group barrier here. A musician five years in gets it at once.
Someone in their first year does not. That is a large share of the readership for a
newsletter about how the money works.
`.com` free

**Break-Even Tour** (Arithmetic)
It names the universal experience: the tour that costs what it makes. Instant recognition,
with some rueful humour that fits the irreverent brief.
*Weakness:* it is a downbeat frame for a publication promising you can do better than break
even. It names the problem instead of the outcome.
`.com` free

**All In the Gate, Cut of the Door, Play for Nothing.** Same territories, all free, all
weaker. `playfornothing.com` is the funniest and the most likely to be misread as advocacy
for playing free.

## Top 3

1. **The Door Split.** The one I would launch. Concrete, in-group, short enough for a handle,
   no format lock. It sounds like a publication rather than a topic.
2. **What the Gig Pays.** The clearest and the most forwardable. Take it if reach matters
   more than insider credibility. Accept the length.
3. **Worth the Gig.** The best compromise and the weakest identity. This is what you pick if
   the other two feel too committed.

**Boldest bet:** Count the Gate. Highest ceiling, narrowest door. It would build a sharper
community faster and grow more slowly.

**Safest bet:** The Door Split. `.com` free, no format lock, no meaningful collision. It
reads correctly whether it becomes a podcast, a course or a book.

## Check these yourself

- **Social handles were not machine-checked.** Platform status codes are wrong in both
  directions often enough to be useless. Reserve on every platform in one sitting. Expect the
  bare handle to be gone on the older ones and plan the `@get-` or `@the-` fallback now.
- Search found no established brand on these exact phrases. "Door split" and "the gate" both
  return generic music-industry results, so expect to compete for the term rather than own it
  immediately. Absence of a brand in search is weak evidence. Plenty of live publications are
  not indexed on their own name.
- No trademark search was run. That is correct at newsletter stage. Do one before you sell
  anything under the name.
