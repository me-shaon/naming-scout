# Naming territories

A territory is a metaphor domain with an internal logic. Once you are inside one, it
generates names on its own, and every name it produces is coherent with the others.

This is the whole method. Permutation searches the space every registrar tool has already
searched. Territories search a space chosen for this brief.

## How to build them for a brief

1. **Strip the brief to concepts.** Mechanism, outcome, felt emotion, enemy, imagery,
   category convention, and the thing it refuses to be.
2. **Ask what each concept is like.** Not synonyms. Analogies from unrelated fields. A
   query profiler *finds the slow thing hidden in a system*: that is diagnostics, that is
   detective work, that is sonar, that is a pit crew.
3. **Keep territories that are far apart.** Four distant territories beat eight adjacent
   ones. If two territories generate similar-sounding names, they are one territory.
4. **Include one territory the user would not have asked for.** The unconventional option
   in the report comes from here, and it is often what they pick.
5. **Name each territory in the user's language**, not as jargon. "Field tools" not
   "instrumentation semantics".

Test a territory before committing to it: can you generate six names inside it in one
minute, and do they sound like each other but not like the other territories? If not, the
territory is too vague.

## Catalogue

Adapt or ignore. A skincare brand and a Kubernetes operator do not share a taxonomy.

**Metaphor and analogy**. The concept borrowed from an unrelated domain.
Epidemiology (contagion, index case, R-naught, superspreader). Forensics (teardown,
postmortem, casefile, dossier). Cartography. Meteorology. Geology. Immunology.

**Transformation**. The change the product causes.
Refine, distil, temper, forge, render, cure, ferment, anneal, alloy, smelt, kiln.

**Motion and pace**. How it feels to use.
Glide, vault, slipstream, cadence, drift, launch, coast, sprint, tailwind, arc.

**Discovery**. Finding what was hidden. Strong for analytics, research, search, security.
Scout, probe, prospect, sonar, beacon, lantern, divining, quarry, trace, sift.

**Reference works**. For anything that teaches or catalogues. Umbrella words here are
heavily mined; the distinctive ones are the specific formats.
Handbook, fieldguide, codex, almanac, canon, compendium, ledger, index, atlas, bestiary,
primer, errata, marginalia.

**Craft and workshop**. Deliberate, made by hand, built to last.
Forge, bench, anvil, lathe, chisel, whetstone, jig, atelier, kiln, foundry, mill.

**Science and instruments**. Precision and measurement.
Calibrate, spectra, isotope, vector, quantum, lumen, parallax, meridian, caliper, assay.

**Nature and organisms**. Growth, resilience, ecosystem behaviour.
Rootstock, mycelium, canopy, tidal, delta, thicket, murmuration, coral, bedrock.

**Navigation**. Direction under uncertainty. Strong for anything advisory.
Compass, sextant, waypoint, azimuth, lodestar, harbour, portage, bearing, chart.

**Competition and games**. Stakes and mastery.
Gambit, endgame, checkmate, parry, wager, longshot, underdog, tackle, sideline.

**Systems and infrastructure**. What everything else runs on.
Substrate, conduit, lattice, backbone, junction, relay, manifold, trunk, spine.

**Architecture**. Structure, thresholds, shelter.
Keystone, buttress, threshold, lintel, cornerstone, portico, scaffold, span, vault.

**Imperatives and phrases**. A name that is an instruction. High brand upside, needs a
tagline to mean anything, and usually available because permutation tools cannot produce them.
`stealthese`, `makeitspread`, `shipsmaller`, `readtheroom`.

**Cultural and idiomatic**. An existing phrase repurposed.
Rule of thumb, dead reckoning, first light, north star, cold open, dry run, house style.

**Playful and irreverent**. Self-aware, for audiences that reward it.
"It blew up", "went viral", "accidentally good". Ages fast; check that the joke survives
the third telling.

**Domain-specific**. The vocabulary of the user's own field, used sideways rather than
literally. Usually the richest territory in the run and the one only the user can supply.
Ask them for five words insiders use that outsiders would not.

**Coined and morphemic**. Latin/Greek roots recombined. Use when every real word is taken
or an ownable trademark matters more than instant meaning. `-ora`, `-ify`, `-um`, `-is`,
`vel-`, `lum-`, `ferr-`. Coined names carry a permanent explanation cost. Justify it.

## Territory health

Track the availability rate per territory across rounds and act on it. From the source
research, checking ~400 names:

| Round | Focus | Available |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Metaphor compounds | 67/150 (45%) |
| 2 | Short brandables, newsletter names | 26/94 (28%) |
| 4 | Umbrella nouns (lab, school, academy, guide) | 11/87 (13%) |
| 5 | Variants on surviving stems | 30/59 (51%) |

Read it like this:

- **Above 40%.** Unmined. Go deeper here.
- **15% to 40%.** Normal. Keep going. Expect to work for the good ones.
- **Below 15%.** Exhausted. The whole market reached for these words. Move on, or go up
  a level of specificity: `guide` is mined out, `fieldguide` is not.

The round-4 collapse and round-5 recovery is the pattern to expect. Generic umbrella nouns
are gone everywhere. Compounds on a distinctive stem still have room.

## Working through a dry territory

Before abandoning one, try:

- **Specify.** `lab` → `wetlab`, `darkroom`, `benchwork`.
- **Change register.** Latinate → Anglo-Saxon, or the reverse. `illuminate` → `lantern`.
- **Move along the process.** Not the tool, but the raw material, the by-product, the
  worker, the workplace, the measurement, the failure state.
- **Take the verb.** Nouns are mined harder than verbs and imperatives.
- **Compound across two territories.** The seam between two territories is the least
  searched space in the whole exercise.

If it is still dry after that, it is genuinely exhausted. Say so in the report: knowing a
direction is closed is a real result.
