# Replace blunt automatic rejection with five-point rule: respelling counts only if it is (1) phonetically identical when spoken, (2) shorter or memorable, (3) not already taken, (4) not a common typo, and (5) the canonical spelling is unavailable on all viable TLDs.

**Question:** Should respelling (character substitution like k→c, vowel doubling) be rejected outright or evaluated case-by-case?

Lyft, Xero, Flickr, Fiverr prove respelling can work, but staticapi case shows it is the first thing everyone tries—respelled variants register within months while canonical spelling often remains free on .io or .dev. Rule shifts focus from blocking to making the leak visible.

**Decided by:** proposed by human human:shaon.cse81@gmail.com, accepted by agent agent:claude-code

## Not taken

- **Ban respelling entirely** (rejected): Rules out successful brands; ignores that the real escape is TLD switching, not spelling.
- **Allow respelling without condition** (rejected): Wastes user time on variants when canonical spelling is free elsewhere; respelled domains register immediately.
